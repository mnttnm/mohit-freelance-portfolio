# Product context

Who the site is for and how it should feel. The visual system (colors, type,
spacing) lives in `DESIGN.md`; this file is the audience and voice behind it.

## Users

Mix of cold and warm traffic. Some discover Mohit through content, social, or search. Others come via referrals and already have a rough idea of what he does. In both cases, the visitor is a founder, product lead, or CTO evaluating whether to reach out. They're busy, they've seen a hundred freelancer sites, and they'll decide in 30 seconds if this person feels like someone they'd want to work with.

## Brand Personality

**Sharp, confident, honest.**

The site should feel like someone who is clearly good at what they do but doesn't need to perform it. No sales language, no marketing speak. Confident enough to be simple. Warm enough to feel approachable. The tone is first-call energy — direct, personal, no pitch deck.

## Design Principles

1. **Say less** — Every word, element, and section must earn its place. If it doesn't help the visitor decide to reach out, it's clutter.
2. **Confidence through restraint** — High-caliber work doesn't need to shout. Let the work and the tone do the talking.
3. **First-call energy** — Everything should read like you'd actually say it to someone. If it sounds like ad copy, rewrite it.
4. **Respect the visitor's time** — These are busy people. Get to the point, make it scannable, make the next step obvious.
5. **Authentic over polished** — Minor imperfections in tone (conversational phrasing, informal structure) signal a real person, not a template.
